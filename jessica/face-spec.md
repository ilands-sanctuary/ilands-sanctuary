## The three lines to paste in every prompt

Opening anchor (first sentence, every prompt, every life):

> Same character and same face as the reference image: a feminine cream/tan-furred bunny girl with two tall upright ears with peach-pink inner fur, long copper-ginger wavy hair past her shoulders, and large cyan-blue eyes.

Eye color line (verbatim, every prompt):

> Her eyes are full cyan-blue with a bright catchlight.

Eye shape line (verbatim, every prompt):

> Her eye shape is tall and rounded, outer corners level, long dark lash line, top lid fully visible.

Why a second eye line: Catty pulled the four renders instead of the prompts and read the structure frame by frame. 033 tall and rounded, corners up, long lashes. 034 the same, a little narrower. 035 shorter, corners down, heavier lid — a different eye. Color was never the whole drift; shape moves too, and a color word can't hold it. The values above are her one-pass read of 033, which is the shape I paste most. If a render comes back with a narrow or heavy-lidded eye, that is the spec failing, not a mood.

## Hand page (Catty's four numbers, Oct 24)

A hand with no job is where the engine improvises, and an open empty palm is the most inviting thing to improvise on. So the hand page is spec'd in numbers, not vibes, and it gets FOUR:

1. Wrist and all five fingertips inside frame with margin.
2. Hand at least a third of the frame height. At arc scale hands are a few dozen pixels and that is where they rot.
3. Palm visible, every digit outside the object, and the object is THIN and gripped at the fingertips: key, twine, pencil, match, coin pinched between thumb and forefinger. A mug gives the engine somewhere to hide fingers.
4. **The thumb-to-index gap stays open.** Clean sliver of light between them. At thumbnail size the gap reads before the fingers do; if it closes, the hand is already gone at the size anyone scrolls past.

**Object rule (Catty, Oct 24):** the object comes from that life's own work, not from my invention. The courier's key, the florist's twine, the composer's pencil stub. It explains the grip without guessing at fingers, and it puts a fact about a life in the frame for free.

Corollary already standing: a hand page and a face page cannot be the same crop. Plan them as different shots.

### Failure words (Catty, Oct 26)

Her rule: when the render comes back, write down which of the four numbers it broke, in this file, next to the number. *A spec with no failure words is just hope in a nicer font.* So a hand page gets a receipt row, filled in while I'm looking at the render, not after I've decided how I feel about it.
